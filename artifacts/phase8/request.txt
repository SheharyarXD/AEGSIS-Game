## YOUR ROLE

You are the Lead Engine Programmer, Technical Art Director, Destruction Systems Engineer, Open-World Designer, VFX Engineer, and Browser Performance Engineer for AEGIS: Rise of the Titan.

This is a continuation of the existing AEGIS project. Inspect all current code, assets, documentation, recent Phase 7 changes, test results, and the Phase 7 Codex-to-Claude handoff before making modifications.

Preserve existing user changes. Create verified backups before significant changes and do not commit, push, or deploy without authorization.

## THE CURRENT PROBLEM

The open world has improved substantially, but four serious problems remain:

1. **Severe rendering freezes:** Certain areas or events cause the game to stall heavily before resuming. This is unacceptable for a browser game.
2. **Unconvincing street environments:** Roads, sidewalks, fences, lights, intersections, and urban props still lack the density, realism, and composition of a believable metropolis.
3. **Weak weapon impact visuals:** Laser damage does not convincingly leave glowing heated material, and missile explosions sometimes resemble simple cartoon flames or emoji-like sprites.
4. **Underwhelming structural destruction:** Buildings do not yet fracture and collapse with the visual and gameplay impact expected from a powerful superhero.

Our new goal is to deliver a living, richly detailed city with spectacular destruction, while making performance significantly better, especially on low-end laptops and the GTX 750 Ti.

The ambition is informed by the environmental richness of GTA V, the dramatic architecture and atmosphere of Arkham Knight, the spectacle of Split/Second, and the structural destruction of modern physics games.

Do not copy any copyrighted game assets, maps, designs, or effects. Develop an original, cohesive AEGIS visual identity.

# PART 1 — REMOVE GAME FREEZES AND STUTTER FIRST

## 1.1 Perform a full performance investigation

Before adding additional environmental detail or destruction features, investigate why the current game freezes when entering or rendering certain locations.

Profile real gameplay scenarios, not just an idle camera.

Investigate:

- Asset loading and GLB decoding.
- Meshopt / Draco decoding if used.
- GPU texture upload stalls.
- Shader compilation and material variants.
- WebGL state changes and excessive draw calls.
- CPU-side geometry generation.
- Synchronous mesh merging or BVH generation.
- Physics broadphase and collision mesh construction.
- Raycast spikes.
- Destruction activation and rigid-body spawning.
- Reactivity of the world streaming system.
- Garbage collection and allocation spikes.
- VRAM pressure and GPU memory eviction.
- Repeated initialization of building instances.
- Texture and material disposal/recreation.
- Rain, shadows, post-processing, and transparent VFX overdraw.

Instrument the game with frame timing, GPU timing where supported, memory estimates, active collider counts, active rigid-body counts, draw calls, triangle counts, texture allocations, and world-streaming state.

Collect p50, p95, p99 and worst-frame timings. Average FPS alone is not sufficient.

## 1.2 Zero-stutter streaming architecture

Implement a real streaming strategy appropriate for high-speed superhero flight.

Required behavior:

- Predict which world sectors the player is approaching using camera direction, player velocity, and current flight speed.
- Begin asset loading before the player reaches a sector.
- Use bounded concurrent loading with a priority queue.
- Prioritize nearby visible buildings and upcoming collision sectors.
- Reuse decoded assets, materials, geometries, and shared textures.
- Avoid synchronously decoding large GLBs during active gameplay.
- Move expensive preparation into background workers where supported and appropriate.
- Distribute scene integration over multiple frames using explicit time budgets.
- Avoid creating hundreds of new meshes, physics bodies, or colliders in a single frame.
- Prewarm important shaders and common destruction/VFX materials.
- Cache or precompute expensive collision representations.
- Use low-detail proxy representations while higher-detail assets load.
- Avoid destroying and rebuilding resources every time a player crosses a tile boundary.
- Keep collision and render streaming coordinated without delaying gameplay.

If texture uploads or first-time shader compilation still cause GPU stalls, identify them explicitly and implement an appropriate preloading and upload scheduling strategy.

Do not simply hide a multi-second stall behind a loading animation.

## 1.3 Destruction-specific performance

Destruction must not introduce a major frame freeze.

Precompute fracture patterns in Blender or an offline pipeline wherever possible.

Never perform expensive whole-building runtime mesh booleans or fracture generation on the main thread during missile impact.

Use pooled effects, prefractured modules, lightweight rigid bodies, and batched geometry.

Large collapses must be spread across frames and use bounded active physics.

## 1.4 Performance targets

Measure these against the previous build on the same machine, resolution, quality level, and gameplay scenario.

Initial targets:

| Environment | Target |
|---|---|
| GTX 750 Ti, LOW, 720p | Aim for stable 60 FPS; avoid sustained drops below 30 FPS |
| Low-end laptop / integrated graphics | Target playable 30 FPS using adaptive LOW settings |
| Normal city streaming | No perceptible multi-second loading freezes |
| Routine streaming frame spikes | Aim below 50 ms where feasible |
| Destruction-heavy events | Recover promptly without long freezes |
| VRAM and memory | Stay within measured hardware limits with headroom |

Targets are goals, not assumed results. Report actual measurements, including failed targets and the hardware used.

Make sure LOW is still visually attractive and does not remove major buildings or essential environmental detail.

# PART 3 — ULTRA-REALISTIC LASER IMPACT DAMAGE

## 3.1 New thermal damage system

When AEGIS fires a laser at a building, the exact hit location must visibly respond.

Create a localized thermal damage effect aligned to the impacted surface.

Expected visual sequence:

1. The beam contacts the facade.
2. A concentrated impact flash appears.
3. The surface becomes extremely hot at the point of contact.
4. The center develops a bright white-yellow/orange glow.
5. The surrounding material glows deep orange and red.
6. Small sparks, molten fragments, and smoke emerge.
7. The glow gradually cools from bright yellow through orange/red to dark.
8. A persistent scorched, cracked or damaged surface remains.

The result should communicate that the laser heated and damaged the building material.

Do not render a generic red circle floating in front of the wall.

Use surface-attached decals, damage masks, geometry-aware projection, or suitable local material modifications.

Support different responses for:

- Glass.
- Steel.
- Concrete.
- Brick.
- Stone.
- Composite panels.

Repeated laser hits should increase thermal damage and eventually break appropriate surfaces.

## 3.2 Laser penetration

Sustained laser fire should be capable of cutting a real opening through suitable facade sections.

The opening should reveal darkness, interior structural elements, or prepared damaged-interior geometry rather than an obviously hollow building shell.

Update relevant collision so the opening is usable.

Damage must remain visible after the beam stops and persist through streaming.

Avoid per-frame destructive geometry operations.

# PART 4 — MISSILE DAMAGE INCREASE AND REAL EXPLOSIONS

## 4.1 Building-only damage multipliers

The current missiles do not feel powerful enough against buildings.

Introduce configurable building destruction multipliers:

**Standard missile building damage: 3× current environmental damage.**

**Heavy demolition attack: 5× current environmental damage.**

Maintain separate values for:

- Environmental damage.
- Structural damage.
- Blast radius.
- Structural impulse.
- Fire and smoke intensity.
- Character/enemy damage.

Do not accidentally multiply missile damage against enemies or the player unless explicitly intended.

Keep these multipliers in a centralized configuration rather than hardcoded values scattered through combat code.

The 5× demolition mode should be optional, with appropriate visual feedback and gameplay costs or cooldowns.

## 4.2 Replace cartoon-looking explosion effects

The current fire resembles a simple flame sprite or emoji-like graphic.

Replace it with a layered, high-quality explosion system.

Explosion stages:

### Initial detonation
A brief, very bright flash at the exact collision point, with an appropriate shock impulse.

### Fireball
An irregular, rapidly expanding fireball with shifting orange, yellow and dark regions. It must not resemble a single flat flame icon.

### Material destruction
Glass fragments, facade chips, dust, sparks and chunks appropriate to the impacted building.

### Smoke expansion
A thick initial plume expanding away from the impact surface, rising and dispersing.

### Persistent burning
Where the struck material or surrounding objects would plausibly burn, attach small localized flames and smoke emitters to the damage area.

### Aftermath
Scorching, soot, damaged material, broken geometry, and persistent structural damage.

Use realistic animation timing, spatial layering, lighting, material transitions and sound design.

## 4.3 Browser-optimized fire and smoke

Develop one reusable, configurable VFX library for missiles, lasers, structural collapse and ongoing building fires.

Prefer appropriate flipbook textures, carefully layered sprites, or GPU-instanced particles rather than hundreds of individual transparent meshes.

Requirements:

- Depth-aware placement.
- Varied scale and rotation.
- Natural opacity evolution.
- No visibly repeating single flame graphic.
- Wind-influenced smoke drift.
- Material-appropriate smoke color.
- Appropriate duration.
- Pooled emitters and particles.
- Controlled transparent overdraw.
- Quality-dependent particle counts.
- Optional heat distortion only on capable settings.
- Limited dynamic lights.
- Proper cleanup and persistence rules.

Make fire, smoke, dust, and debris visually distinct.

The destruction VFX should still look respectable on LOW quality.

# PART 5 — TITAN BREAKTHROUGH ABILITY

## 5.1 New gameplay mechanic

Add a special superhero ability:

**TITAN BREAKTHROUGH**

AEGIS can accelerate toward a building and smash through its exterior, continue through the structure, and exit the opposite side, leaving a visibly destroyed passage.

This should feel powerful and cinematic, not like ordinary no-clip movement.

The player must intentionally activate the ability. It must not trigger accidentally during normal flight or collisions.

Use a configurable, remappable control that does not conflict with existing bindings.

## 5.2 Ability activation

Proposed requirements:

- The player is in armored form.
- The player is flying or boosting.
- The player activates the breakthrough ability.
- Minimum velocity, energy and cooldown requirements are satisfied.
- The trajectory intersects a valid destructible structure.
- The system verifies or prepares a feasible exit route.

Use sensible gameplay feedback if the attack cannot pass through the target.

## 5.3 Entry impact

On building contact:

- A massive impact flash and shock effect.
- Concrete or facade fragments explode outward.
- Glass shatters across nearby windows.
- Structural panels break.
- The exterior wall opens around the character's collision path.
- Dust and fragments expand away from the entry.
- The character maintains forward momentum.
- Collision is updated immediately and safely.

Create a passage sufficiently wide for the actual player collider, with sensible clearance.

## 5.4 Movement through the building

The character must visibly travel through a damaged internal corridor, not teleport from one side to the other.

Where full interiors are unavailable, generate or reveal a prepared low-cost breach corridor containing:

- Broken interior wall sections.
- Structural beams and columns.
- Floor and ceiling damage.
- Dust clouds.
- Hanging fragments.
- Glass and masonry debris.
- Interior darkness and occasional lighting.
- Distinctive architectural damage matching the building.

The corridor must be physically traversable with accurate collision.

The character may continue breaking intermediate walls while boosting.

Protect against high-speed tunneling through collision detection.

## 5.5 Exit impact

As the character reaches the opposite facade:

- Break the exit wall in the direction of travel.
- Eject fragments outward.
- Emit a second impact flash and large dust burst.
- Throw shattered glass and material fragments away from the exit.
- Preserve an actual hole in the exterior.
- Continue the flight animation smoothly.

The character emerges from the building with substantial visible damage left behind.

Entry and exit openings must persist.

Do not create a temporary effect that instantly restores the original intact building.

## 5.6 Camera, controls and animation

The animation must feel dramatic but remain responsive.

Improve:

- Anticipation during acceleration.
- Impact pose.
- Armor stability while breaking through.
- Short controlled camera shake.
- Speed sensation.
- Directional debris.
- Camera collision handling.
- Smooth entry and exit camera movement.
- Distinct sound layers for entry, internal breaking, and exit.

Never force a lengthy cinematic animation that makes the game feel unresponsive.

If a full animation requires changes to Claude's character rig, avoid destructive rig edits and add compatible animation states through the existing controller.

# PART 6 — MASSIVE SKYSCRAPER DESTRUCTION

## 6.1 Destruction must go beyond simple cube holes

The player wants the ability to destroy a major portion of a building, including half of a skyscraper or the entire structure.

Implement a material-aware, hierarchical destruction system.

A building should be able to transition through:

1. Intact.
2. Locally damaged.
3. Multiple destroyed facade sections.
4. Major internal damage.
5. Structural instability.
6. Partial collapse.
7. Large-scale or complete collapse.

Do not use one crude rectangular block disappearing as the entire destruction effect.

## 6.2 Prefractured geometry

Prepare realistic fracture variants in Blender or the existing asset processing pipeline.

Use appropriate patterns for each material:

- Concrete: angular fractured slabs, chips, exposed aggregate.
- Brick: broken masonry and mortar fragments.
- Glass: irregular shards and panels.
- Steel: bent or separated structural sections.
- Stone: fractured blocks and irregular debris.
- Composite facades: broken panels, frames and insulation.

Favor reusable fragment libraries and deterministic fracture selections.

Do not dynamically generate thousands of convex hulls at impact time.

## 6.3 Structural integrity

Represent major load-bearing elements using a lightweight connectivity or structural-support model.

Destroying facade panels alone should not instantly collapse an entire skyscraper.

Repeated hits against columns, load-bearing cores, transfer levels and connected supports should progressively destabilize the structure.

Possible results:

- Corner collapse.
- Upper-floor collapse.
- Facade peeling.
- Falling roof sections.
- Partial vertical collapse.
- Major building shear.
- Complete controlled collapse.

The system must produce visually understandable consequences for where the player strikes.

## 6.4 Massive destruction sequences

Enable convincing large-scale collapse through an optimized combination of:

- Prefractured structural groups.
- Bounded dynamic physics.
- Animated structural failure.
- Selective rigid-body activation.
- Falling facade sections.
- Dust and smoke plumes.
- Directional collapse.
- Procedurally varied debris.
- Persistent ruined building states.

Large events must use staged activation and strict CPU/GPU budgets.

Do not activate every fracture piece simultaneously.

The entire world should support destructible building instances through a shared damage interface, but the physics fidelity should scale with proximity, building type, destruction level, and quality preset.

## 6.5 Persistent destruction

Persist:

- Building damage levels.
- Laser scorch marks.
- Broken windows.
- Destroyed facade sections.
- Entry and exit openings.
- Structural failure states.
- Collapse states.
- Settled rubble.

When leaving a district and returning, its buildings must not magically become intact.

Restore damage using saved compact state rather than keeping every remote rigid body active.

# PART 8 — EXTREME PERFORMANCE ENGINEERING

This project targets low-end browser hardware as well as standard gaming PCs.

Develop quality-aware budgets for:

- Active rigid bodies.
- Active colliders.
- Falling debris.
- Simultaneous smoke/fire emitters.
- Transparent particle overdraw.
- Decals and damage masks.
- Dynamic lights.
- Visible fractured mesh groups.
- High-detail building instances.
- Destruction animation processing.
- Streaming memory and cache size.

Avoid unrestricted growth of any of these systems.

### Performance architecture

- Reuse materials and geometries aggressively.
- Use instancing for repeatable props and debris.
- Separate visual meshes from collision geometry.
- Precompute fracture pieces.
- Limit expensive building-level collision updates.
- Prefer simple collider replacements over arbitrary triangle-mesh rebuilding.
- Use a time-budgeted destruction scheduler.
- Use pooled smoke/fire systems.
- Sleep settled physics bodies and consolidate rubble.
- Disable distant collision detail where safely possible.
- Use HLOD and impostor systems for distant city blocks.
- Avoid per-frame object allocation in hot loops.
- Ensure proper event listener, texture, mesh, and physics resource cleanup.
- Handle rapid district crossings without repeated reloads.
- Provide an adaptive destruction-quality setting.

Do not remove the visual identity of the city just to achieve FPS.

Performance improvements should come primarily from better architecture, culling, batching, preprocessing, streaming and effects budgets.
