AEGIS — WORLD REALISM, STREET DETAIL, LIGHTING, AND DESTRUCTION VFX REBUILD
You are now focusing specifically on making the AEGIS city feel like a believable, premium, realistic urban world.
The current city is still too artificial, too empty, too flat, and too game-like.
It does not yet feel like a real place.
I want you to upgrade the world itself with a strong focus on:
- realism,
- render quality,
- New York–style street design,
- small environmental details,
- better roads and sidewalks,
- better lights,
- denser believable urban dressing,
- and realistic destruction smoke/fire when buildings are hit.
This work must remain compatible with the current AEGIS gameplay, flight, combat, destruction, and browser deployment goals.
1. MAIN GOAL
Make the city feel like a real urban environment, especially at street level.
It should feel inspired by:
- New York City block structure,
- dense American downtown streets,
- realistic sidewalks,
- grounded road systems,
- believable building entrances,
- layered city lighting,
- premium urban atmosphere.
Do not create a copyright copy of NYC or any real map.
Use it only as urban design inspiration.
2. STREET-LEVEL REALISM — TOP PRIORITY
The player must feel like they are inside a real city when walking or hovering near the ground.
Roads
Rebuild and improve roads so they look like real urban streets.
Include:
- realistic asphalt materials,
- lane striping,
- turning lanes where useful,
- faded paint variation,
- crosswalks,
- stop lines,
- patched road sections,
- manholes,
- drainage grates,
- curb edges,
- road wear,
- subtle cracks,
- oil and dirt variation,
- puddle accumulation in wet conditions.
Avoid flat blank roads with almost no detail.
Sidewalks / footpaths
I want much better footpath language, closer to real New York style.
Add:
- modular sidewalk slab pattern,
- curb transitions,
- curb ramps,
- utility covers,
- grates,
- storefront edge detail,
- steps / stoops where suitable,
- subtle wear,
- stains,
- corner treatment,
- sidewalk width variation by district,
- transitions to plazas and alleys.
The sidewalks must no longer feel like plain gray filler planes.

Lighting must become much more realistic and cinematic.
During the day
Improve:
- sun direction and contrast,
- facade readability,
- better ambient light balance,
- more believable shadows,
- stronger urban form definition.
During dusk / night
Improve:
- street lighting,
- building window lighting,
- warm/cool contrast,
- wet pavement reflections,
- intersection lighting,
- darker but still readable streets,
- stronger depth and atmosphere.
During rain / storm
Improve:
- wet material response,
- subtle reflections,
- street light reflection streaking,
- moody visibility,
- stronger atmosphere without making gameplay unreadable.
Avoid fake over-bloomed visuals.
The goal is realism, not exaggerated effects.

Buildings must feel grounded into the city.
Improve:

6. WORLD COMPOSITION IMPROVEMENT
The city still feels too scattered and too synthetic.
Refine the world layout so it feels more like a believable city:
- stronger block structure,
- more coherent road network,
- better district transitions,
- more convincing density,
- less empty dead space,
- better skyline layering,
- more realistic spacing between buildings.
Preserve the district identities, but make them feel like parts of one coherent metropolis.
7. REALISTIC DESTRUCTION VFX
This is a major focus.
When buildings are hit, the destruction must produce realistic fire and smoke, not fake blocky effects.
On missile impact
When a missile hits a building, add:
- an initial impact flash,
- sparks and debris,
- realistic smoke burst,
- small persistent flame on the damaged side,
- dark scorch mark,
- embers or glowing fragments if appropriate,
- facade dust,
- glass shatter where relevant.
On repeated hits
As damage increases:
- smoke becomes thicker,
- flames spread slightly around the impact area,
- scorch darkening increases,
- debris accumulation grows,
- the damaged section looks more burned and unstable.
On major structural damage
When a section heavily breaks:
- use larger smoke plumes,
- dust clouds,
- falling fragments,
- brief fire bursts,
- and then a believable lingering smoke/fire state.
Important realism rule
Fire and smoke must feel attached to the impact side of the building.
Do not place generic floating explosion effects unrelated to the damaged geometry.
8. REALISTIC SMOKE
Smoke quality matters a lot.
I want smoke that feels:
- thick when needed,
- directional,
- naturally dissipating,
- darker at the core,
- lighter at the edges,
- reactive to major impacts,
- scalable by quality level.
Use efficient, pooled, optimized smoke systems suitable for browser deployment.
Avoid:
- cheap constant puffs,
- repetitive identical particles,
- smoke that vanishes too suddenly,
- giant opaque blobs.
9. REALISTIC FIRE
Fire should feel like real localized burning.
It should:
- flicker naturally,
- stay attached to impact zones,
- vary in size based on damage,
- cast subtle light if technically feasible,
- not flood the scene with expensive effects,
- remain optimized for web performance.
Small missile hits should create smaller flames.
Larger destruction moments can create more intense fire.
10. PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENT

This must still target browser playability and ideally scale toward platforms like Poki/CrazyGames.
So while upgrading realism, you must also:
- optimize road materials,

- use LODs,
- batch repeated street assets,
- reuse materials,
- limit particle overdraw,
- pool smoke/fire effects,
- reduce expensive dynamic lights,
- keep quality presets functional.

Do not trade massive FPS loss for modest visual improvement.
