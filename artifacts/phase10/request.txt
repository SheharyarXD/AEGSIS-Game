# PART A — REBUILD Y AS A FULL CINEMATIC ABILITY

## A1. Fundamental behavior change

The current Phase 9 implementation fires a stationary 3/3/2 missile barrage, creates a traversable corridor and then leaves traversal to the player.

That behavior must now be upgraded.

### New ability: TITAN BREACH — CINEMATIC ASSAULT

The intended sequence:

**Press Y → Target Lock → Armor Deployment → Missile Barrage → Entry Destruction → AEGIS Automatically Accelerates → Cinematic Building Traversal → High-Speed Exit Explosion → Return Player Control.**

This is one uninterrupted, choreographed attack.

The player should NOT have to manually press W, Shift or other movement controls to travel through the building.

The player should NOT have to reach a minimum speed beforehand.

Y must work when AEGIS is:

- Standing on the street.
- Standing on a rooftop.
- Hovering motionless.
- Flying slowly.
- Already flying toward a target.

The character may automatically transition into powered flight as part of the ability.

While the cinematic is running, regular movement and weapon input should be temporarily disabled or appropriately buffered.

Normal gameplay must resume after the sequence finishes.

Preserve the separate O/F whole-building demolition system.

## A2. Before starting the animation

The ability must verify:

- Armored mode is active.
- A valid destructible building is targeted.
- Sufficient energy is available.
- Launchers are ready.
- Entrance and exit points are feasible.
- The building can produce a player-sized traversable passage.
- Collision and damage geometry can be prepared.
- The intended exit does not intersect an invalid world boundary or another solid building.

Use actual building geometry, collision information and prepared destructible sections.

Do not begin the cinematic and then discover that the player cannot pass through the building.

If the path is invalid, reject activation with a clear HUD notification.

Existing energy requirements and cooldowns should be preserved or deliberately rebalanced.

# PART B — CINEMATIC ANIMATION STORYBOARD

The following timing is an initial creative target. Adjust after reviewing actual gameplay.

Total target duration: approximately 5–7 seconds.

The sequence must feel cinematic without leaving the player waiting unnecessarily.

## Shot 1 — Target Acquisition

Approximate duration: 0.4 seconds.

Camera:

- Smoothly shift toward an over-the-shoulder composition.
- Frame AEGIS and the target building together.
- Slightly narrow field of view.
- Display a precise tactical targeting overlay over the intended breach area.

Animation:

- AEGIS turns toward the structure.
- Upper torso and helmet align.
- Shoulders stabilize.
- Chest reactor and weapon channels brighten subtly.
- Missile targeting markers acquire multiple structural points.

Audio:

- Low mechanical servo movement.
- Tactical targeting confirmation.
- Short digital lock tone.
- Subtle reactor power increase.

Do not completely obscure the screen with excessive HUD effects.

## Shot 2 — Missile System Deployment

Approximate duration: 0.5 seconds.

Create convincing mechanical deployment:

- Shoulder armor panels separate.
- Missile launch assemblies extend.
- Small mechanical locks release.
- Launch rails rotate toward the target.
- Armor-mounted lights activate.
- Character adopts a powerful firing posture.

Use the existing rig, preserving AEGIS's original armored silhouette.

Where necessary, create properly authored animations rather than simple instant transforms.

Use layered animation blending and smooth pose transitions.

## Shot 3 — Coordinated Missile Barrage

Approximate duration: 1.2–1.8 seconds.

Preserve the existing eight-missile capability as the initial default.

Missiles should fire in multiple coordinated waves, not all materialize at once.

Recommended starting choreography:

- Wave 1: three missiles attack the outer facade.
- Wave 2: three missiles penetrate the building's prepared internal breach corridor.
- Wave 3: two missiles clear the far-side exit.

Each missile must:

- Launch from a visible weapon socket.
- Have convincing acceleration and exhaust.
- Follow a physically coherent trajectory.
- Avoid visibly passing through intact surfaces.
- Produce an impact at its actual collision point.
- Damage real building sections.

Use subtle timing variation to avoid robotic synchronisation.

Camera should follow the barrage through a controlled blend of close and wider views.

Avoid excessive rapid cuts.

## Shot 4 — Entrance Destruction

Approximate duration: 0.5 seconds, overlapping with the barrage.

The entrance facade must break convincingly.

Required effects:

- Irregular fractured wall sections.
- Glass shattering.
- Large outward fragments.
- Concrete chips.
- Dust.
- Sparks.
- Directional smoke.
- Localized fire where plausible.
- Structural frames visible beneath the facade.

Create a player-sized opening matching the actual path.

The opening must not resemble a clean rectangular cutout.

Use source-derived damage geometry and prefractured sections with believable material edges.

## Shot 5 — AEGIS Acceleration and Automatic Entry

Approximate duration: 0.6 seconds.

After the entry is confirmed clear, AEGIS launches toward the opening automatically.

Animation:

- Character shifts into an aggressive forward flight pose.
- Back thrusters flare.
- Palm and boot thrusters activate appropriately.
- Energy channels intensify.
- Body aligns with the prepared trajectory.
- Character rapidly accelerates toward the entry.

Camera:

- Begin with a dramatic rear three-quarter angle.
- Blend into a close chase perspective.
- Briefly emphasize acceleration using field-of-view changes and restrained motion blur where available.

The character must physically move through the existing world.

Do not teleport between entrance and exit.

## Shot 6 — Interior Destruction Traversal

Approximate duration: 1–1.5 seconds.

This is the most important new cinematic section.

AEGIS must visibly travel through the building.

Create or reveal prepared interior damage geometry appropriate to the building type.

Examples:

### Modern office skyscraper
- Broken glass partitions.
- Suspended ceiling fragments.
- Steel framing.
- Office floor plates.
- Interior columns.
- Hanging cable trays.
- Shattered cladding.
- Dust illuminated by exterior light.

### Gothic architecture
- Broken stone vaulting.
- Fragmented pillars.
- Heavy masonry.
- Stone dust.
- Damaged arches.
- Structural timber or appropriate internal supports.

### Industrial building
- Broken piping.
- Steel beams.
- Service platforms.
- Mechanical fixtures.
- Concrete fragments.
- Industrial dust and sparks.

The player should experience a sequence of interior structural impacts rather than a dark, empty corridor.

Important:

- Use optimized prepared interior geometry.
- Do not require complete furnished interiors for every building.
- Reveal additional detail only along the active breach corridor.
- Keep surrounding undamaged building geometry intact where appropriate.

During traversal, use a cinematic flight animation with small impacts, armor stabilization, directional debris, and appropriate camera shake.

## Shot 7 — High-Speed Exit Explosion

Approximate duration: 0.6–0.9 seconds.

This is the climax of the ability.

As AEGIS reaches the opposite facade:

1. The far wall fractures.
2. AEGIS strikes the final weakened surface.
3. A massive directional burst of glass, concrete and steel erupts outward.
4. A bright impact flash appears.
5. Dust and smoke expand from the new opening.
6. AEGIS emerges at very high speed.
7. Thruster exhaust intensifies.
8. The camera swings into a dramatic exterior reveal.
9. The character clears the building and transitions into normal flight.

The exit should feel significantly more powerful than the entrance.

Create a visible shockwave and a brief directional explosion synchronized to the superhero's emergence.

The blast must not be represented by a flat flame icon.

Use an original multi-layered particle and shader effect.

Consider a brief, subtle time-ramp as AEGIS bursts out, but never make controls feel unresponsive.

## Shot 8 — Cinematic Recovery

Approximate duration: 0.4 seconds.

The camera returns smoothly to the gameplay camera.

Restore full controls.

Preserve the player's momentum, but provide a controllable safe initial velocity so the character does not immediately collide with another building.

The HUD returns to the normal flight layout.

The building continues releasing smoke and falling debris behind the player.

Do not snap the camera or character orientation abruptly.

# PART G — PERFORMANCE: PREMIUM LOOK WITHOUT HEAVY RENDER COST

This is a browser game intended for platforms such as Poki and CrazyGames.

The game must provide attractive visuals on modest hardware.

The developer's current GTX 750 Ti has 2 GB VRAM.

MEDIUM is the primary quality reference.

### Targets

Aim for:

- Approximately 60 FPS during normal MEDIUM gameplay at 720p where feasible.
- Playable 30+ FPS on appropriately configured lower-end systems.
- Consistently responsive camera and input.
- No multi-second world-streaming freezes.
- No major main-thread stalls during cinematic activation.
- Stable destruction with bounded debris and VFX.
- Reasonable GPU memory headroom.

These are aspirational targets. Measure actual results and disclose failures.

### Required techniques

Use:

- Predictive asset streaming.
- Shader prewarming.
- GLB preprocessing.
- GPU-compressed textures.
- Shared materials.
- Instancing.
- Batched meshes.
- Block-level HLOD.
- Distance-based geometry and material detail.
- Simplified collision hulls.
- Baked environmental lighting where appropriate.
- Limited dynamic lights.
- Selective shadow updates.
- Pooled fire and smoke.
- Limited transparent overdraw.
- Precomputed fracture meshes.
- Preloaded cinematic breach interiors.
- Time-budgeted scene graph updates.
- Dynamic resolution when necessary.
- Strict memory cleanup.
- Controlled debris activation.

Do not calculate arbitrary mesh fractures during gameplay.

Do not let the new cinematic force the engine to load large interior meshes synchronously after Y is pressed.

Preflight and preload the necessary corridor assets before committing to the animation.

### Quality tiers

LOW:

MEDIUM:
- Primary visual target.

ULTRA:
