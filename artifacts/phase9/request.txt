# PHASE 9 — TOTAL DEMOLITION, TITAN BREACH BARRAGE & LIVING METROPOLIS
## Complete implementation specification for Codex

You are the principal game-engine programmer, destruction systems engineer, senior technical artist, animation director, urban environment designer, and performance engineer working on AEGIS: Rise of the Titan.

This is a real implementation task. Do not just write a plan or demonstrate simulated results.

Work directly within the existing AEGIS game, inspect the latest Phase 8 handoff and verification records, preserve previous working systems, and implement the changes described below.

The project currently uses Three.js, Blender-authored architectural assets, Rapier-based physics, and an existing missile/laser/flight/destruction system.

# 1. UNDERSTAND WHAT PHASE 8 GOT WRONG

The previous implementation made technical progress, especially in streaming and frame times. However, the intended gameplay is still not achieved.

Critical problems:

1. The Y breakthrough ability requires player movement and flight alignment. This is fundamentally different from the requested feature.
2. Demolition does not reliably destroy an entire major building through normal player-fired missiles.
3. Full-collapse demonstrations relied on scripted support damage instead of genuine weapon input.
4. Some destruction still resembles sections disappearing rather than a physically convincing structural failure.
5. Smoke, explosions and fire lack adequate visual sophistication.
6. Buildings have simple and repetitive interiors, with weak exposed destruction surfaces.
7. Roads, footpaths and urban environments are not sufficiently dense or believable.
8. The world still lacks the small details that make a metropolis feel inhabited.
9. Performance testing does not fully establish smooth simultaneous explosions and city destruction in dense scenes.

Do not treat passing automated tests as visual acceptance.

# 2. PRIMARY GAMEPLAY CHANGE — REBUILD THE Y ABILITY

## New name: TITAN BREACH BARRAGE

**Y must no longer require the player to fly forward, boost or hit a building physically.**

This is a stationary, animated, missile-based architectural breaching ability.

The player may activate it while:

- Standing on a road.
- Standing on a rooftop.
- Hovering in place.
- Flying at low speed.
- Remaining completely stationary.

The player must not need to move at high speed.

### Intended behavior

AEGIS faces a building and presses Y.

The character enters a powerful attack animation.

Shoulder and forearm weapon systems deploy, target locks appear over the building, and a coordinated series of missiles launches.

The missiles strike the facade and internal structure in stages, producing a real destruction corridor through the building.

The missiles complete the entrance-to-exit passage.

Only after the passage is physically clear does the HUD display:

**BREACH COMPLETE — PATH OPEN**

The player then chooses whether to walk, run, hover or fly through that opening.

Do not automatically move the character through the building.

Do not teleport the character.

Do not disable character collision to fake traversal.

# 3. TITAN BREACH — ANIMATION DIRECTOR SPECIFICATION

Create a cinematic but responsive multi-stage animation.

## Stage A — Target acquisition

Duration guideline: approximately 0.3–0.6 seconds.

When Y is pressed:

- Identify the targeted building using the actual camera/weapon targeting system.
- Check range, line of sight and target validity.
- Calculate an entrance point on the visible facade.
- Calculate a feasible path through the building.
- Calculate the exit point on the opposite side.
- Determine which wall, facade, floor or structural modules must be destroyed.
- Reserve necessary collision changes and destruction resources.

Display subtle targeting graphics that outline the expected breach corridor.

If the target is invalid, show a clear reason and do not consume ammunition.

## Stage B — Armor weapon deployment

Duration guideline: approximately 0.5–0.8 seconds.

Create a convincing character animation:

- Shoulder missile compartments open.
- Mechanical launcher rails deploy.
- Armor panels articulate where the rig supports it.
- The player assumes a stable firing posture.
- Small servo and targeting sounds play.
- Weapon indicator lights activate.
- A short multi-target lock sequence appears.

Use the existing AEGIS skeleton and animation infrastructure.

If needed, author additional animation clips using Blender.

Do not replace or damage the existing hero model.

## Stage C — First missile wave: Entrance destruction

Launch approximately 3–4 guided missiles.

The missiles must visibly leave their launchers.

Give each projectile an individually readable flight path, with varied but controlled timing.

They should strike the selected facade in a coordinated pattern.

Impact effects:

- Bright initial flash.
- Physically plausible directional explosion.
- Breaking glass.
- Shattering facade panels.
- Falling wall fragments.
- Sparks.
- Pressure dust.
- Smoke.
- Localized fire where appropriate.

The initial wave opens the entrance.

It must reveal actual damaged geometry and a passable opening, not simply a black decal.

## Stage D — Second missile wave: Interior penetration

Launch an additional 3–5 guided missiles.

They should travel through the newly created entrance or use an equally physically coherent route.

They strike the internal corridor in stages.

Destroy the required:

- Interior partitions.
- Floor-to-ceiling obstructions.
- Nonessential facade modules.
- Structural panels appropriate to the building's design.

Show successive shock waves and dust eruptions progressing inward.

Prepare the corresponding interior breach geometry offline whenever possible.

## Stage E — Final missile wave: Exit destruction

Launch another coordinated wave of approximately 2–4 missiles.

These missiles must reach the far-side breach target through an unobstructed created corridor or a visible exterior guided route.

Do not let missiles invisibly pass through intact solid concrete.

Destroy the exit facade and expose the exterior world.

The player should be able to see daylight, streetlights or scenery through the entire passage.

## Stage F — Aftermath animation

- Missile compartments retract.
- Smoke escapes from the new openings.
- Smaller debris continues to fall.
- Fire persists in appropriate damaged areas.
- Broken facade edges remain.
- The HUD confirms the corridor is clear.

The character returns control to the player immediately.

If attack choreography takes too long, optimize its timing while preserving the visual sequence. Aim for a satisfying sequence of a few seconds, not a lengthy unskippable cutscene.

# 4. REAL PHYSICAL PASSAGE THROUGH BUILDINGS

The Titan Breach system must produce a true navigable corridor.

### Geometry requirements

Calculate the passage based on the existing player collider, with comfortable clearance.

A useful initial target is approximately 5–7 meters wide and 4–6 meters high, adjusted to actual character dimensions and building structure.

A corridor may pass through multiple prepared interior modules, depending on building depth.

The opening must not be restricted to a single wall.

### Visual requirements

The player should see:

- Fractured concrete edges.
- Exposed steel or reinforcement.
- Broken glass frames.
- Damaged floor slabs.
- Collapsed interior wall pieces.
- Exposed ceiling infrastructure.
- Hanging cables and fragments.
- Dust floating through the opening.
- Scorching and smoke around missile impact points.
- The exterior environment visible through the exit.

Use different damage materials for glass skyscrapers, Gothic stone buildings, offices and industrial architecture.

### Collision requirements

The opening must have matching collision geometry.

Update affected colliders after each destruction stage.

Verify a continuous player-sized route from entrance to exit before displaying BREACH COMPLETE.

Preserve normal collision everywhere outside the destroyed corridor.

Do not let the player fall through intact-looking floors.

### Persistence

If the player leaves the district and returns, both openings and the damaged corridor must remain.

Support saving and reloading damage state through existing persistence infrastructure.

If the path cannot be prepared safely, reject the ability before firing rather than creating a visually broken, impassable corridor.

# 5. TOTAL DEMOLITION — ENTIRE BUILDING COLLAPSE

This is the second major gameplay requirement.

**Normal player-fired demolition weapons must be capable of destroying an entire skyscraper.**

The existing 5× building damage is not sufficient if the destruction system cannot translate it into major structural failure.

Rework damage propagation instead of merely increasing an HP multiplier.

## 5.1 Demolition mode

Preserve the existing O demolition toggle.

When demolition mode is active, F should fire heavy structural-destruction missiles.

Introduce a configurable high-damage demolition profile that targets:

- Facade integrity.
- Structural support integrity.
- Load-bearing columns.
- Core strength.
- Floor connections.
- Section connectivity.

As an initial tuning experiment, test structural damage substantially beyond the current 5× profile, potentially equivalent to 10–15× the original environmental damage.

These are starting values, not fixed final balancing requirements.

**The acceptance requirement is much more important than the numeric multiplier: full building collapse must actually happen through player-fired attacks.**

## 5.2 Expected demolition power

Tune the system so that:

- Small buildings can collapse from one well-placed heavy demolition barrage.
- Medium buildings can collapse after one or two strong barrages.
- Large skyscrapers can undergo catastrophic progressive collapse after a concentrated sequence, ideally no more than about three major demolition barrages at vulnerable structural sections.

The player should not need to fire 50 missiles into a skyscraper just to remove a few wall panels.

However, the result must still look like structural damage rather than a building being deleted instantly.

## 5.3 Structural destruction graph

Develop or upgrade a lightweight authored structural dependency system.

Use grouped structural nodes:

- Foundation / lower supports.
- Main load-bearing core.
- Perimeter support columns.
- Floor plates.
- Vertical facade sections.
- Roof structure.
- Attached architectural components.

Damage should propagate according to support dependencies.

Destroying enough lower supports should cause overlying sections to lose stability.

The building should collapse in a visibly coherent sequence.

This is an artistic structural simulation, not a claim of engineering-grade real-world structural physics.

## 5.4 Progressive collapse animation

A skyscraper collapse should include:

1. Initial structural damage and visible cracks.
2. Distortion or breakup of important support sections.
3. Localized floor or facade failure.
4. Large facade sections falling outward.
5. Connected floors progressively losing support.
6. Upper building sections tilting, dropping or telescoping downward as appropriate.
7. Large rolling dust clouds.
8. Falling glass, concrete and steel.
9. Significant ground impact.
10. Secondary debris and localized fire.
11. A persistent ruined structure.

**The building must not disappear, scale down, sink through the ground, or turn into a small stack of rectangular blocks.**

Use multiple collapse archetypes appropriate to building construction.

## 5.5 Realistic final ruins

Final destruction should visibly occupy much of the original building footprint.

Prepare LODs for ruined buildings.

Include:

- Major broken structural masses.
- Exposed building cores.
- Broken floor stacks.
- Concrete rubble.
- Steel framing.
- Glass fragments.
- Crushed facade sections.
- Dust and soot.
- Localized flames.
- Persistent collision on major ruins.

Smaller debris may consolidate into static rubble after settling.

The ruins must still look substantial on MEDIUM quality.

# 6. IMPORTANT SEPARATION OF ABILITIES

Titan Breach and Demolition are different gameplay features.

| Feature | Y — Titan Breach | O + F — Demolition |
|---|---|---|
| Player movement required | No | No |
| Missiles used | Coordinated multi-wave barrage | Heavy structural missiles |
| Main objective | Open a navigable passage | Destroy a building |
| Expected result | Entrance, interior and exit openings | Partial or total structural collapse |
| Player automatically moves | Never | Never |
| Structure preserved | Where feasible | Can be completely destroyed |

Do not merge both features into one vague damage animation.

A Titan Breach should not automatically demolish the entire target building unless a separate structural failure is legitimately triggered.

# 12. LASER, MISSILE, FIRE AND SMOKE REALISM

Preserve Phase 8 improvements but increase visual fidelity.

## Laser

A sustained laser should:

- Heat the impact surface.
- Produce white-hot and orange-red contact glow.
- Scatter sparks.
- Create surface smoke.
- Gradually darken into a persistent burn mark.
- Eventually penetrate suitable materials.

The glow must be attached to the correct geometry.

## Missiles

Missile detonations should use multiple visual stages:

1. Initial intense flash.
2. Expanding irregular fireball.
3. Directional blast and flying fragments.
4. Dust and debris.
5. Smoke expansion.
6. Localized lingering fire where plausible.
7. Persistent damage and soot.

Do not use a static flame picture or emoji-like fire sprite.

Create better optimized flipbook sequences, shader-controlled irregular fire shapes, volumetric-looking layered smoke, and pooled particle effects.

Real volumetric simulation is not required if high-quality approximations look convincing.

## Massive collapse VFX

The demolition system requires a separate large-scale effects profile:

- Directional ground dust.
- Floor-by-floor dust emission.
- Rolling collapse clouds.
- Secondary facade impacts.
- Heavy debris audio.
- Material-specific particles.
- Controlled camera shake.
- Large smoke plumes.
- Localized persistent fire.
- Ruined-building ambient effects.

Fire should not cover the entire building uniformly unless justified by actual damage.

# 13. MEDIUM-QUALITY PERFORMANCE IS THE MAIN TARGET

Treat MEDIUM as the primary development and visual-quality preset.

The user wants a rich environment without the game becoming unplayable.

**Do not design the game for ULTRA first and then strip the entire world down for MEDIUM.**

MEDIUM should have:

- High-quality nearby building facades.
- Complete street architecture.
- Detailed sidewalks.
- Functional road markings.
- Useful prop density.
- Convincing night lighting.
- Clear missiles and laser damage.
- Visually substantial debris and ruins.
- Good smoke and explosion effects.
- Smooth district streaming.

Optimize the implementation underneath.

## Performance requirements

Prioritize:

- Shared materials and texture atlases.
- Compressed runtime textures.
- Geometry instancing.
- Hierarchical LOD.
- Block-level HLOD and impostors.
- Precomputed damaged meshes.
- Prefractured building components.
- Limited dynamic rigid bodies.
- Baked or approximated lighting.
- Shadow caster budgets.
- Camera visibility culling.
- Predictive world streaming.
- Pooled effects.
- Low-allocation update loops.
- Time-budgeted destruction processing.
- Worker-based offline or background preparation where appropriate.
- Aggressive cleanup and GPU-memory management.

Prevent large frame spikes when an unfamiliar building or destruction effect becomes visible for the first time.

## Performance acceptance goals

On the actual GTX 750 Ti:

- Target around 60 FPS during ordinary MEDIUM gameplay at 720p where feasible.
- Keep heavy combat and demolition playable, ideally remaining above 30 FPS.
- Avoid freezes exceeding 100 ms during normal streaming or missile activation.
- Limit expensive physics activation during whole-building collapse.
- Maintain stable camera controls during major demolition.

These are engineering targets. Measure real results and report failures.

Also provide LOW configurations for low-end laptops and browser portal deployment.

Avoid visual shortcuts that make the city empty just to improve average FPS.
